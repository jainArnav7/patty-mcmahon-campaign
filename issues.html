Issues

Simple summaries of McMahon's stated positions on key issues.

Political Ideology

Patty McMahon is running as a Libertarian. Her campaign emphasizes congressional oversight, constitutional limits on government power, civil liberties, and opposition to unnecessary military intervention.

McMahon also argues that strong party divisions can weaken voters' voices. She says representatives should put their constituents ahead of party agendas and consider good ideas regardless of which party proposes them. Her work as an EEOC congressional liaison, including 25 years working with congressional offices, informs her focus on communication and bridging differences.

Civil Rights & Liberties

McMahon says people should have due process, free speech, privacy, and equal treatment under the law. In simple terms, the government should follow fair rules and respect people's rights.

Her campaign flier says she supports equal treatment and civil marriage under the law, and opposes banning same-sex marriage based on religion. It says she supports sex-based rules for women's sports and spaces and opposes irreversible medical procedures for minors. On abortion, the flier says she supports legal protection after a fetal heartbeat is detected, limits on federal power over personal moral questions, and public funding for some health care when kept separate from abortion funds.

The campaign materials reviewed here do not give clear positions on affirmative action, police body cameras, immigration, or mass incarceration. We should not guess what she thinks about those topics.

Economy, Taxes, and Government Spending

McMahon says Congress should check the president's power and watch how federal money is used. She supports looking into whether government agencies spend money for the reasons Congress approved.

Her campaign proposes the TRUST Act. It would help make sure federal money is used for its approved purpose and protect workers who follow the law. She also wants Congress to debate military action and says war should be a last choice.

Her campaign page does not give specific plans for tax cuts, inflation, tariffs, gas prices, jobs, or the budget deficit.

Education

McMahon proposes O.P.E.N. America, an online learning program for adults. It would offer affordable classes that could help people earn certificates, learn job skills, or continue their education.

The campaign page does not give specific positions on K–12 funding, Common Core, school vouchers, student loans, or school choice.

Environment and Energy

The campaign information reviewed here does not give specific positions on climate change, energy, farming, air or water pollution, alternative fuels, or national parks. We should not guess what McMahon thinks about these topics.

Health Care

Her campaign flier says she supports public funding for family planning, cancer screenings, and preventive care, as long as that money is kept separate from funds used for abortions. It also says she supports protecting embryos made through assisted reproduction.

The campaign materials reviewed here do not give specific plans for health insurance costs, mental health, prescription prices, Medicare, Medicaid, or health care for all.

Defense and Security

McMahon says war should be a last choice and should have a clear reason and approval from Congress. The campaign information reviewed here does not give specific positions on guns, school safety, domestic terrorism, Ukraine, Iran, or a border wall.

Social Issues

Her campaign flier gives positions on abortion, same-sex marriage, and rules for women's sports and spaces. It says she supports legal protection after a fetal heartbeat is detected, opposes banning same-sex marriage based on religion, and supports sex-based rules for women's sports and spaces. It also says she opposes irreversible medical procedures for minors.

The campaign information reviewed here does not give clear positions on the death penalty, poverty, crime, housing, homelessness, or ICE. It also does not give a clear position on every civil-rights topic listed in the assignment.
