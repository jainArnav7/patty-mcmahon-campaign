District and Voter Strategy

Reaching Voters Across CO-6

A district-focused plan for local outreach, clear information, and voter feedback.

Where the Campaign Would Focus

The main message would be: “Freedom. Accountability. A Government That Answers to You.” This message connects McMahon's focus on personal rights with her plan for Congress to watch how government works.

This is a congressional-district race, so the campaign's focus is Colorado's 6th District rather than a battleground state. The district includes Aurora, Littleton, Centennial, and parts of Adams, Arapahoe, Jefferson, and Douglas counties, as well as a small portion of Denver. The Colorado Public Radio voter guide provides an overview of the district.

Colorado's 2024 election abstract counted 529,478 registered voters in the District 6 congressional contest: 446,259 in Arapahoe County, 49,665 in Jefferson County, 28,390 in Adams County, 4,221 in Douglas County, and 943 in Denver. These are 2024 figures, not a current registration count.

Aurora and Arapahoe County

This is the largest of the listed county portions by voter count. The campaign would prioritize community events and locally distributed materials, with clear explanations of McMahon's positions on congressional oversight, civil liberties, and education.

Littleton, Centennial, and Nearby Suburbs

Local news, community meetings, and campaign flyers could introduce McMahon's federal-spending oversight proposals and O.P.E.N. America, her plan for affordable online adult education.

Other Parts of the District

In the portions of Adams, Jefferson, and Douglas counties and Denver, the campaign would arrange meet-and-greets and share district-specific online updates. That helps ensure outreach is not limited to the largest population centers.

Who the Campaign Would Reach

The 2024 American Community Survey estimate for the district was 730,108 residents. The estimated median household income was $103,252, 8.3% of residents were below the poverty line, about 48% of adults had a bachelor's degree or higher, and 24.7% of residents spoke a language other than English at home. These are districtwide estimates; they do not describe every neighborhood or individual. The figures are available in Census Reporter's Congressional District 6 profile.

Outreach would be open to voters across party affiliations, with messages organized around issues rather than assumptions about a person's identity. Unaffiliated and cross-party voters could hear how McMahon wants Congress to check presidential power. Working adults and people exploring career training could learn about O.P.E.N. America through libraries, community colleges, workforce centers, and online education pages. People interested in civil liberties, government oversight, or foreign policy—including veterans and military families—could attend public question-and-answer events and review sourced explanations of her positions.

Because many residents speak a language other than English at home and the district includes people with different education and income levels, materials should use plain words and be offered in relevant languages where possible. The campaign would invite all communities to take part and use voluntary surveys to learn which issues residents want addressed. It would not guess someone's views based on race, religion, gender, or another identity.

Traditional and Social Media

Local television, radio, newspapers, and community events would introduce McMahon and give residents a chance to hear from her directly. The campaign would seek interviews on local news and public-affairs programs to explain her background and answer questions from across the district.

As a proposed television buy, the campaign could run fifteen 30-second ads on Denver- and Aurora-area television or cable channels during local news and public-affairs programming. One ad would introduce McMahon and her experience; others would explain her views on Congress's role, civil liberties, and federal spending. These topics match her published platform and give voters clear reasons to learn more.

Online, short videos and sourced posts on Facebook, Instagram, X, and TikTok could explain the same topics, share event information, and link to longer details for people who want them. Content about O.P.E.N. America could be shared through libraries, community colleges, workforce centers, and online education pages. The campaign's positive and attack ads are linked on this site.

Polling to Understand Voter Priorities

The campaign could survey about 400 registered voters using a mix of phone calls and online responses. Recruitment should cover different geographic parts of the district, rather than drawing responses from one neighborhood alone. Under a simple random-sample assumption, 400 responses have an approximate margin of error of plus or minus five percentage points at a 95% confidence level. Nonresponse, weighting, and other sampling limits can make the actual margin larger.

Questions would be neutral and ask which issues Congress should prioritize; how important topics such as spending, civil liberties, education, health care, and jobs are; where voters get candidate information; what they want to know about McMahon's positions; and whether the campaign has overlooked an issue. An optional written response would let participants raise other concerns. Results would be reported in aggregate, personal information would not be published, and the findings would help the campaign decide which topics need clearer explanations in interviews, ads, and social media.

AP U.S. Government campaign project for Patty McMahon, Libertarian candidate for U.S. House, Colorado's 6th District.
